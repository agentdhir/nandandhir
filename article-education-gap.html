Why Pharma Graduates Aren't Job-Ready — And What IChrom Is Doing About It

Dr. Nandan Kumar Dhir
January 2026 6 min read Education

India graduates over 200,000 pharmacy students every year. Yet, when I speak with quality directors and laboratory heads across the pharmaceutical sector, their most pressing challenge remains the same: finding talent that is actually ready to work in a GMP-regulated laboratory environment.

The paradox of the Indian pharmaceutical industry is that while we are the "pharmacy of the world," our academic institutions are struggling to produce graduates equipped with the practical skills required to sustain that title. The gap between what is taught in classrooms and what is expected on the shop floor has never been wider.

The Anatomy of the Skills Gap

When an entry-level analyst joins a pharmaceutical QC laboratory, the learning curve is exceptionally steep. The deficiency is rarely in theoretical knowledge — graduates can recite the principles of partition chromatography perfectly. The failure points are practical and behavioral:

1. Zero Hands-On Instrumentation Experience

High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) is the workhorse of pharmaceutical analysis. Over 70% of analytical testing in a standard QC lab involves HPLC or GC. Yet, the vast majority of B.Pharm and M.Sc. Chemistry graduates have never independently operated an HPLC system. They have seen diagrams in textbooks, perhaps watched a demonstration from a distance, but they lack the tactile experience of:

  • Preparing and degassing mobile phases correctly
  • Purging pumps and establishing baseline stability
  • Troubleshooting pressure fluctuations or baseline drift
  • Interpreting a chromatogram beyond basic retention time matching

2. The "GMP Documentation" Blind Spot

Academic laboratory notebooks are designed to record academic outcomes. Industry documentation is designed to survive a USFDA audit. Graduates are entirely unprepared for the rigors of Good Documentation Practices (GDP) and ALCOA+ principles. They do not understand that in industry, if it is not documented contemporaneously, it is a rumor.

3. A Compliance-Agnostic Mindset

In academia, a 95% yield is an A grade. In industry, an Out-of-Specification (OOS) result triggers a massive cross-functional investigation. Graduates lack the fundamental understanding of "Quality Culture" — the realization that their analytical result directly impacts patient safety and organizational compliance.

The Industry Cost

Pharmaceutical companies currently spend 6 to 9 months re-training fresh graduates before they can be trusted to run independent analyses. This training burden slows down lab operations and increases the risk of compliance failures during the onboarding period.

The IChrom Solution: Simulating the Industry

Having observed this gap throughout my 27-year career leading quality operations at Ranbaxy, Glenmark, and Torrent, I founded the IChrom Chromatography Research Lab. Our mission is not to replace academic education, but to complete it.

IChrom is designed to function exactly like an industry QC laboratory. We don't just teach the theory of chromatography; we immerse students in the reality of pharmaceutical analysis.

Immersive Hands-On Training

Our training programs guarantee that every student gets direct, independent time on modern analytical instruments (HPLC, UPLC, GC). They learn by doing:

  • Setting up analytical sequences
  • Performing system suitability testing (SST)
  • Executing actual assay and related substances methods
  • Encountering and troubleshooting real instrument errors

GMP Environment Simulation

At IChrom, students don't use spiral-bound notebooks. They work with industry-standard SOPs, controlled worksheets, and strict adherence to ALCOA+ principles. We teach them that an overwritten value without a date, signature, and justification is an integrity violation, not a simple mistake. They learn the weight of their signature.

Bridging the Gap Through Academia Partnerships

We actively partner with leading institutions — including Chandigarh University, Punjabi University, MM University, and SLIET Punjab — to integrate practical industrial exposure into the academic journey. Through expert talks, workshops, and extensive industrial visits, we give students a clear line of sight into what their future employers will demand.

IChrom graduates reduce industry onboarding time by up to 70%

A Call to Action for the Industry

The responsibility for closing the skills gap does not rest solely on academic institutions. The pharmaceutical industry must shift from being passive consumers of talent to active developers of it.

We need more structured apprenticeship programs. We need industry leaders to spend time in academic advisory councils. We need pharmaceutical companies to donate decommissioned (but functional) HPLC systems to universities so students can practice.

"Quality cannot be inspected into a product, and competence cannot be inspected into a graduate. Both must be built by design from the foundation up."

— Dr. Nandan Kumar Dhir, Founder, IChrom

Conclusion

The future of the Indian pharmaceutical industry depends on the quality of the talent operating its laboratories. By transitioning from purely theoretical education to hands-on, GMP-aligned practical training, we can create a workforce that is not just highly educated, but truly job-ready.
